# Gyoza — Steamed

Soft, steamed gyoza with a bright dipping sauce, rebuilt from a meal at Kyohana in Kyoto. The sauce's brightness tastes like yuzu, but it's actually a small splash of apple cider vinegar. That's the key detail. The filling below is a standard juicy pork-and-cabbage one. The juiciness comes from salting and squeezing the cabbage, then beating liquid back into the pork until it binds.

Makes about 40.

## Ingredients

### Filling

- ½ small head napa cabbage (about 3 cups finely chopped)
- 1 tsp salt, for the cabbage
- 1 lb ground pork, not lean (20–30% fat)
- 3–4 green onions or a small bunch of garlic chives (nira), finely chopped
- 1 Tbsp grated ginger
- 2–3 garlic cloves, grated
- 1 Tbsp soy sauce
- 1 Tbsp sake
- 2 tsp toasted sesame oil
- ¼ tsp white pepper
- 1 tsp potato starch or cornstarch
- 3–4 Tbsp cold chicken stock or water
- 40 round gyoza wrappers

### Sauce

- 3 Tbsp soy sauce
- 1 Tbsp mirin
- 1–2 tsp apple cider vinegar (a small splash; start with 1)
- ½ tsp grated ginger
- ½ small garlic clove, grated
- 1 green onion, thinly sliced

## Instructions

1. **Salt the cabbage.** Toss the chopped cabbage with the salt and let it sit 15–20 minutes. Wring it hard in a towel. You'll get a surprising amount of water out, and that's what keeps the gyoza from going soggy.
2. **Beat the filling.** Combine the pork, soy, sake, sesame oil, white pepper, and starch. Stir hard in one direction with chopsticks or your hand for a minute or two until it turns sticky and pale. Then beat in the stock a tablespoon at a time until it's absorbed. This is what makes the filling juicy.
3. Fold in the cabbage, green onion, ginger, and garlic.
4. **Fill and pleat.** Put a heaping teaspoon of filling in the center of each wrapper, wet half the edge with water, and fold, pleating the front edge 5–6 times against the flat back. Seal firmly and squeeze out air. Keep finished gyoza on a starch-dusted tray under a towel.
5. **Make the sauce** while you fill so it has time to sit. Stir everything together. Taste: it should be savory and round with a clean lift at the end. Add more vinegar a few drops at a time if it isn't bright enough, but stop before it reads as vinegar.
6. **Steam.** Line a steamer basket with parchment (poke a few holes) or napa cabbage leaves. Steam the gyoza in a single layer, not touching, over boiling water for 8–10 minutes, until the wrappers turn translucent and the filling is firm.
7. Serve right away with the sauce.

## Notes

- **Freezing.** Freeze uncooked gyoza on the tray until solid, then bag them. Steam from frozen for 12–14 minutes.
- **Boiled instead (sui gyoza).** Drop into boiling water, and once they float, cook 2–3 minutes more. They come out even softer.
- The sauce shares its base of soy, mirin, ginger, garlic, and green onion with the sauce for [crispy chicken with ponzu and pink peppercorn](../entrees/east-asian/chicken-crispy-ponzu-pink-peppercorn.md). There, yuzu supplies the acid instead.
