# Sun tea

## Ingredients

- 2 L water
- 9? sachets tea (see notes)
- Sweetener to taste, optional (see the sweetening chart below)

## Instructions

1. Fill a glass jug with 2 L of water.
2. Add the tea to the water.
3. Put a loose lid on the container.
4. Put the container in direct sunlight for two hours.
5. Move the container to the shade for a few hours.
6. Sweeten to taste while the tea is still warm from the jug, if sweetening at all.
7. Bring inside, chill, ice, and enjoy.

## Sweetening

Amounts are for the full 2 L batch. Sweeten toward the low end of a row, chill, then taste and adjust — cold suppresses perceived sweetness noticeably, so a pitcher that tastes right at room temperature reads flat straight out of the fridge. It's much easier to add than to subtract.

| Sweetness level      | Granulated sugar | Honey         | Agave nectar   | Sugar per 8 oz glass |
| -------------------- | ---------------- | ------------- | -------------- | -------------------- |
| Barely there         | ¼ cup (50 g)     | 2 Tbsp (40 g) | 1½ Tbsp (32 g) | ~6 g                 |
| Semi-sweet           | ½ cup (100 g)    | ¼ cup (85 g)  | 3½ Tbsp (72 g) | ~12 g                |
| Sweet                | 1 cup (200 g)    | ½ cup (170 g) | 7 Tbsp (145 g) | ~24 g                |
| Southern-diner sweet | 1½ cups (300 g)  | ¾ cup (255 g) | ⅔ cup (215 g)  | ~36 g                |

The three columns are matched for equal sweetness, not equal volume. Honey runs roughly 15–20% sweeter than granulated sugar by weight and agave about 40% sweeter, both from their higher fructose content, so each one needs less than the column to its left. The last column is what a single glass ends up carrying — the "sweet" row lands at about the sweetness of a cola, which is what bottled sweet tea actually is.

Notes on method and flavor:

- Sun tea never gets hot enough to dissolve granulated sugar, so stirring it into the jug just leaves a grainy layer on the bottom. Dissolve it in a cup of hot water first, or keep a 1:1 simple syrup on hand and pour to taste. Agave dissolves readily in cold liquid; honey wants a splash of warm water to loosen before it will disperse.
- Agave is neutral and stays out of the tea's way. Honey brings its own floral, grassy note that plays well with black tea and with lemon. Plain sugar is the clean, traditional profile.
- Acid eats sweetness. If lemon is going in, nudge the sweetener up a notch from where it tasted right unsweetened.

## Notes

- I always use Harney & Sons Paris tea which is a delightful Earl Grey with blackcurrant. It comes in sachets which is a double tea bag. Adjust the recipe accordingly if you're using normal tea bags or measuring out loose leaf tea.
