# Jeera Rice

Cumin-scented basmati. The workhorse Indian rice — what you want next to [dal](../entrees/curries/masoor-dal-red-lentil.md) or anything not deeply aromatic on its own.

Serves 2–3.

## Ingredients
- 1 cup basmati rice (aged Indian — India Gate, Tilda, Daawat, Lal Qilla)
- 1¼ cups water (after soaking; see Method)
- 1 tsp ghee
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- ½ tsp salt
- 1 bay leaf (optional)

## Method

Rinse the rice in cool water, swirling and pouring off, until the water runs mostly clear — usually 5–7 changes. Then soak in cool water for 20–30 minutes. Drain well.

Optional but better: toast the cumin seeds in ghee in a small pan over medium heat for ~20 seconds, just until aromatic. This wakes up the spice more than dropping it in cold. If you're feeling lazy, skip and just add everything cold to the cooker; still good.

Add the drained rice, water, ghee, cumin, salt, and bay leaf (if using) to the Zojirushi. Run the white rice setting. Fluff with a fork or rice paddle when it's done — don't stir aggressively or you'll break the grains.

## Notes

- **Water ratio matters.** Zojirushi water lines are calibrated for Japanese short-grain. For soaked basmati, you want ~1:1.25 by volume, not the 1:2 the line implies. Ignore the line, measure by eye.
- If the rice comes out dry, bump to 1:1.5 next time. Different brands behave a little differently.
- Don't use Mahatma or other American-grown "basmati-style" rice — wrong soil, wrong aging, wrong aromatics. Get the real stuff from an Indian grocery; bulk bags are cheap.
- Bay leaf is optional but free flavor if you have one.
- Picking out the bay leaf and unbroken cumin seeds isn't necessary; just eat around them.
