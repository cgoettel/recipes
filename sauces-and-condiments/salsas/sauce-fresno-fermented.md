# Salsa — Fresno (Fermented)

A lacto-fermented hot sauce in the modern craft template: red Fresno chiles, carrot for body, garlic for depth, onion for round. Funky, savory, layered — fundamentally different from a fresh-blended sauce. Yields about 24 oz. Active time is roughly 30 minutes; fermentation runs 2–4 weeks.

## Ingredients

- 1 lb (~450 g) fresh red Fresno chiles, stems removed
- 4 oz (~115 g) carrots, peeled
- 1 small white or yellow onion (~4 oz / ~115 g), peeled
- 6–8 cloves garlic, peeled
- 25 g kosher salt (~2.5% of total vegetable weight)
- Filtered (non-chlorinated) water, as needed
- Optional: 1–2 T white wine vinegar at the end

## Instructions

1. Roughly chop the chiles (leave seeds in for full heat; scrape out for milder), carrots, onion, and garlic into ~½" pieces so they pack into the jar.
1. Weigh everything in a non-reactive bowl. Note the total weight, then add salt at 2.5% of that weight (about 25 g for the quantities above). Mix well and let sit 15 minutes so the salt pulls liquid out of the vegetables.
1. Pack the mixture firmly into a quart wide-mouth mason jar, pressing down to release more liquid. Leave 1½" headspace at the top.
1. Add filtered water to cover the solids by ½", keeping 1" headspace.
1. Place a fermentation weight on top to keep everything submerged. Cap with an airlock fermentation lid (or a regular lid you'll burp daily for the first week).
1. Ferment at room temperature (65–75°F is ideal) for 2–4 weeks:
   - Days 1–5: very active. CO₂ bubbling, possibly some foam at the top. This is correct.
   - Week 1–2: activity slows, brine cloudy, smell pleasantly funky-sour.
   - Week 2–4: deepens. Taste with a clean spoon at week 2 and decide whether to keep going. Longer = more complex.
1. When done, strain the solids and reserve the brine. Blend the solids, adding brine until the consistency is right (most hot sauces are thinner than the first blend looks — keep adding). Optionally strain through a fine sieve for a smoother texture.
1. Stir in the optional vinegar if using. Bottle into clean glass bottles and refrigerate. Keeps for many months.

## Notes

**Equipment.** A quart wide-mouth mason jar, a fermentation weight (glass disc, or a sandwich bag of brine sealed and dropped on top), an airlock fermentation lid (Pickle Pipe, Easy Fermenter, or basic airlock + grommet kit), a kitchen scale, and a blender. All on Amazon for under $30 total if you don't have any of it.

**Heat level.** Fresnos run 2,500–10,000 SHU — about the same as a jalapeño at the high end. Leaving seeds in lands closer to 10k; scraping them lands closer to 2.5k. Add a few hotter chiles (cayenne, serrano, even a single habanero) to push heat without losing the Fresno character.

**Salt percentage.** 2.5% is the standard sweet spot for vegetable ferments: high enough to inhibit unwanted bacteria, low enough to let Lactobacillus thrive. 2% works but sits closer to the risk edge; 3% slows the ferment and the result tastes saltier. Don't go below 2% or above 3.5%.

**Chlorinated water kills the ferment.** Municipal tap water has chlorine that inhibits the bacteria you need. Use filtered, bottled, or tap that's been boiled and cooled. Distilled is fine too.

**Kahm yeast vs mold.** A thin white film on the brine surface is kahm yeast — harmless but tastes off; skim it off if it appears. Actual mold (fuzzy, colored — green, blue, black, pink) means start over. The difference is texture: kahm yeast is flat and smooth; mold is fuzzy and three-dimensional.

**Skipping the vinegar.** A successful ferment will have a pH below 4.6 from lactic acid alone, which is shelf-safe in the fridge. The final vinegar is for brightness and an extra safety margin, not strictly required. Skip it for a purer fermented character.

**Aging.** Improves over 1–3 months in the fridge as flavors integrate. Keeps 6+ months easily; chiles soften, garlic mellows, acidity rounds out.

**Variations.**

- **Serrano + carrot + garlic** — same template, hotter, more grassy.
- **Cayenne + carrot + garlic** — same template, leaner, sharper.
- **Smoked element** — replace 2–3 oz of the Fresnos with chipotle morita (smoked jalapeños), rehydrated briefly in hot water before adding. Adds depth and a deliberately Tabasco-adjacent direction.
- **Aromatic add-ins** (sparingly, at the start): a few peppercorns, a small bay leaf, 1 t fresh oregano, a strip of lime peel.
