# Gyoza dipping sauce

A bright gyoza dipping sauce, rebuilt from a meal at Kyohana in Kyoto, where it came with soft steamed gyoza. The brightness tastes like yuzu, but it's actually a small splash of apple cider vinegar. That's the key detail. Good frozen gyoza plus this sauce gets most of the way to the restaurant plate.

## Ingredients

- 3 tablespoons soy sauce
- 1 tablespoon mirin
- 1–2 teaspoons apple cider vinegar (a small splash; start with 1)
- ½ teaspoon grated ginger
- ½ small garlic clove, grated
- 1 green onion, thinly sliced

## Instructions

1. Stir everything together and let it sit 15 minutes or more so the ginger and garlic infuse.
2. Taste: it should be savory and round with a clean lift at the end. Add more vinegar a few drops at a time if it isn't bright enough, but stop before it reads as vinegar.

## Notes

- **Steaming frozen gyoza.** Line a steamer basket with parchment (poke a few holes) or napa cabbage leaves, set the gyoza in a single layer not touching, and steam from frozen over boiling water for 10–14 minutes, until the wrappers turn translucent. Or boil them: once they float, cook 2–3 minutes more. Both come out soft, the way Kyohana served them.
- The sauce shares its base of soy, mirin, ginger, garlic, and green onion with the sauce for [crispy chicken with ponzu and pink peppercorn](../../entrees/east-asian/chicken-crispy-ponzu-pink-peppercorn.md). There, yuzu supplies the acid instead.
