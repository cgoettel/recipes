Baker’s percentages

bread

Bakers write recipes as ratios instead of cups. Flour is always 100%, and every other ingredient is its weight as a percentage of the flour’s weight. A dough with 500 g flour and 350 g water is 70% water.

Ratios travel. The same percentages make one loaf or twenty, and they tell you what kind of bread you’re making before you mix anything. A recipe that says “70% hydration, 2% salt, 1% yeast” has already told you a lot.

Why weigh

A cup of flour weighs anywhere from 120 to 150 g depending on how you scoop it. That swing alone moves a dough from 65% to 80% hydration. Weights take the guesswork out. The bread recipes here carry gram estimates marked ≈, converted from volume; a real weighing beats them.

Working it out

  1. Add up all the flour (every kind counts toward the 100%).
  2. Divide each other ingredient’s weight by the flour weight, times 100.
  3. To make a different amount, pick the flour weight you want and multiply each percentage by it. Or pick a total dough weight: divide it by the sum of all the percentages.

Example: 500 g flour, 350 g water, 10 g salt, 5 g yeast is 100% + 70% + 2% + 1% = 173%. For a 900 g dough, flour is 900 ÷ 1.73 ≈ 520 g, water 364 g, salt 10 g, yeast 5 g.

What each ratio does

Hydration

All the water in the dough as a share of flour; milk counts at about 87%, since that’s how much of it is water. More water means a looser dough, a more open crumb, and a dough that’s harder to shape.
