# Kaga bōcha curry rice

A Japanese curry made with Kaga bōcha (加賀棒茶), the roasted stem tea from Kanazawa. Bōcha is a kind of hōjicha made from the stalks instead of the leaves. It's toasty and nutty, with almost no bitterness. In a curry it does the same job as long-browned onions and dark roux: it adds a toasted bass note that makes the sauce taste deeper without tasting like tea.

The tea goes in twice: brewed strong into the cooking liquid, and ground into the roux. Serve it the Kanazawa way, which means thick and dark on a steel plate, with shredded cabbage on the side and usually a katsu on top.

## Ingredients

- 25–30 g Kaga bōcha (about ¾–1 cup loose; any good hōjicha works), plus 1–2 teaspoons more, ground to powder
- 1–1½ L chicken stock, ideally the [Kyohana-style giblet stock](curry-rice-kyohana-style.md)
- 2–3 large yellow onions, thinly sliced
- 2 tablespoons neutral oil
- 1½ lb chicken thighs or pork shoulder, in large bite-size pieces
- 3–4 garlic cloves, grated
- 1" piece ginger, grated
- 1 tablespoon tomato paste
- ¾ stick (6 tablespoons) butter
- ½ cup flour
- 3–4 tablespoons curry powder (S&B, or the homemade blend from the [Kyohana-style curry](curry-rice-kyohana-style.md))
- 1–2 tablespoons soy sauce
- 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce
- Salt to taste
- Optional: 1–2 carrots and 1–2 Yukon Gold potatoes, in large chunks

### To serve (Kanazawa style)

- Steamed short-grain rice
- Finely shredded green cabbage
- Pork or chicken katsu, sliced
- Tonkatsu sauce, drizzled over the katsu

## Instructions

1. **Brew the tea stock.** Bring the stock to about 195°F (just below a simmer). Take it off the heat, add the loose bōcha, and steep 4–5 minutes, then strain, pressing the leaves. Stems are forgiving, but don't stretch the steep past 6–7 minutes or tannins creep in. Taste it: it should smell strongly of roasted tea, about twice drinking strength.
2. **Caramelize the onions** in the oil over medium to medium-low heat for 45–60 minutes, until deep mahogany. Deglaze with a splash of tea stock whenever the bottom catches.
3. **Brown the meat.** Season with salt and brown hard in a separate skillet, then add to the onions. Deglaze the skillet with tea stock and pour that in too.
4. Add the garlic, ginger, and tomato paste to the onions and cook 2–3 minutes, until the paste darkens.
5. **Make the roux.** Melt the butter in a small pan, whisk in the flour, and cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, until it reaches a dark peanut-butter or milk-chocolate brown (15–20 minutes). Kanazawa curry is very dark, so push the roux further than you would for a standard Japanese curry, but stop if it smells scorched. Off the heat, stir in the curry powder and the ground bōcha.
6. Warm 1 L of the tea stock in the onion pot and whisk in the roux a ladle at a time until smooth. Blend with an immersion blender if you want the classic smooth, glossy sauce.
7. Add the vegetables if using. Simmer gently, uncovered, 30–45 minutes, stirring the bottom often. Kanazawa curry is thick enough to hold its shape on the plate, so let it reduce and add more stock only if it starts sticking.
8. Season with soy sauce, Worcestershire, and salt.
9. Serve on rice with a mound of shredded cabbage beside it and sliced katsu laid over the curry.

## Notes

- **Finding bōcha.** Look for Maruhachi Seicha's Kaga bōcha online or at Japanese grocers. Any roasted hōjicha substitutes well. Stem-heavy ("kuki") hōjicha is closest.
- **Leftover leaves.** The spent leaves are still toasty. Grind dried spent leaves with flaky salt for a tea salt to sprinkle on the katsu.
