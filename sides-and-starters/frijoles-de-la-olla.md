# Frijoles de la Olla

Adapted from Gordo Rancho's frijoles de la olla, scaled to 250 g of beans — enough for several meals. A pot of beans cooked slowly in their own broth: a bowl on its own with the caldo, or the base for refried beans and taco nights.

Serves several meals for one, or 2–3 as a side.

## Ingredients

- 250 g dried runner beans (or pinto), soaked overnight
- 1–2 tbsp olive oil
- ½ white onion, sliced
- 2–3 garlic cloves, minced
- water to cover
- salt

## Method

Soak the beans overnight in plenty of cool water — no salt. Salt this early toughens the skins and slows softening. Drain.

In a heavy pot, sauté the sliced onion in olive oil over medium heat until translucent and just starting to brown. Add the garlic and cook until aromatic, ~30 seconds — don't let it scorch.

Add the drained beans and cover with at least an inch of fresh water. Bring to a hard, aggressive boil and hold it there for 15 minutes — the rolling boil up front helps the beans cook evenly.

Drop to a low simmer, partially covered, and let it go 3–4 hours, until the beans are fully soft and the broth has turned silky. Top up with hot water any time it threatens to drop below the beans.

Salt to taste only in the last 30 minutes, once the beans are already tender.

## Notes

- No salt during the soak or early cooking — it firms the skins and stalls softening. Salt once the beans are tender, not before.
- The long, low simmer is where the broth (caldo) develops body. Don't push the heat to hurry it; you'll split skins before the insides go creamy.
- Runner beans (ayocote) are big and meaty; pintos are the classic and cook a little faster. Either works.
- Eat as a bowl with the broth, or drain and use as the base for refried beans. Day 2 is better.
- Sized for a few meals — double everything for a crowd.
