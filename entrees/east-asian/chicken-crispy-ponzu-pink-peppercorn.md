# Crispy chicken with ponzu and pink peppercorn

Rebuilt from a meal at Kyohana in Kyoto. It's in the family of yurinchi, the Japanese-Chinese dish of fried chicken under a sharp soy and green-onion sauce, but here the acid comes from yuzu, and crushed pink peppercorns add a floral, resinous lift. The chicken is double-fried in potato starch so the crust survives the sauce.

## Ingredients

### Chicken

- 1½–2 lb boneless, skin-on chicken thighs
- 1 tablespoon soy sauce
- 1 tablespoon sake (or water)
- 1 teaspoon grated ginger
- 1 garlic clove, grated
- ½ teaspoon salt
- ¾–1 cup potato starch (katakuriko); cornstarch works but is less crisp
- Neutral oil for frying

### Sauce

- 3 tablespoons soy sauce
- 1½–2 tablespoons yuzu juice (bottled is fine), or swap the soy and yuzu for 4–5 tablespoons ponzu
- 1–1½ tablespoons mirin
- 1 teaspoon grated ginger
- 1 small garlic clove, grated
- 3–4 green onions, thinly sliced, white and green parts
- 1–1½ teaspoons pink peppercorns, lightly crushed

## Instructions

1. **Make the sauce first** so the aromatics have 30 minutes or more to infuse. Stir everything together except half the green onion and half the peppercorns, which go on at the end. Taste: it should be salty-sour first, with the mirin only rounding the edges. If the mirin is noticeable, it's too much.
2. **Marinate briefly.** Cut each thigh into 2–3 large pieces, or leave them whole and slice after frying. Toss with the soy, sake, ginger, garlic, and salt for 20–30 minutes. Longer makes the surface wet and the crust gummy.
3. **Dredge.** Pat off excess marinade and press each piece into the potato starch, especially the skin side. Let them sit 5 minutes until the starch looks damp in patches. That hydrated starch is what turns glassy-crisp.
4. **First fry** at 325–340°F for 4–5 minutes, until just cooked through and pale gold. Rest on a rack for 4–5 minutes. Carryover heat finishes the inside and pushes moisture to the surface.
5. **Second fry** at 375°F for 1–2 minutes, until deep golden and crackly. Drain on the rack, not on paper towels, which steam the bottom.
6. **Sauce at the table.** Slice the chicken if whole, plate it, and spoon the sauce over just before serving so part of the crust stays crisp and part soaks it up. Finish with the reserved green onion and pink peppercorns.

## Notes

- The sauce is a close cousin of the [gyoza dipping sauce](../../sauces-and-condiments/sauces/dipping-sauce-gyoza.md). That one swaps the yuzu for a splash of apple cider vinegar.
- Pink peppercorns aren't true pepper. Crush them lightly between your fingers or with the flat of a knife, since grinding them fine turns them dusty.
