# Pickles — Hot Spears

A jalapeño-spiked refrigerator dill pickle in the Grillo's hot spears school. Same template as [Pickles — Dill](pickles-dill.md); just adds heat. Yields one quart jar.

The dill recipe carries the technique details (cucumber sourcing, blossom-end trim, tannin trick, no-heat rule, brine ratios). Same rules apply here.

## Ingredients

- 1 lb Kirby/pickling cucumbers
- 1½ C filtered water
- 1½ C distilled white vinegar (5%)
- 2 T kosher salt
- 1 t sugar (optional)
- 4–6 cloves garlic, peeled and lightly smashed
- 2–3 fresh dill heads (or 4–5 dill fronds)
- 1–2 black tea bags, OR 2–3 grape/oak leaves
- 3–4 fresh jalapeños, sliced into rounds (seeds in for full heat; scrape for milder)
- 1 t red pepper flakes
- Optional: 1 t whole black peppercorns, 1 t whole coriander seeds

## Instructions

1. Trim ⅛" off the blossom end of each cucumber. Halve into spears (the canonical Grillo's hot-spears cut).
2. Combine water, vinegar, salt, and sugar in a bowl; stir to dissolve. Do not heat.
3. Pack the cucumber spears tightly into a wide-mouth quart mason jar, interspersing the garlic, dill, sliced jalapeños, red pepper flakes, tannin source, and any optional spices.
4. Pour the cold brine over to cover, leaving ¼" headspace. Weight or wedge to keep everything submerged.
5. Cap with a regular lid and refrigerate.
6. Wait at least 48 hours. Heat infuses faster than dill flavor — noticeably spicy at 24 hours, fully integrated at 3–4 days, peak around a week.

## Notes

**Heat level.** 3–4 jalapeños with seeds in lands solidly spicy but not overwhelming — Grillo's hot spears territory. For more heat: add a habanero slice or two (1–2 should be plenty), or substitute serrano for jalapeño. For less: scrape the jalapeño seeds, drop to 2 jalapeños, or skip the red pepper flakes.

**Heat carries through the brine too.** The pickling brine of a hot batch becomes its own ingredient — splash into bloody marys, into ranch dressing, over fried chicken. Save the jar liquid after the spears are gone.

**Variations.**

- **Smoky** — add a chipotle morita (smoked jalapeño) to the jar along with or instead of fresh jalapeños. Different character; deeper, more savory.
- **Mixed heat** — half jalapeño + half serrano for layered heat profiles.
- **Garlic forward** — bump garlic to 8–10 cloves for a fierier garlic-and-heat profile (Grillo's "Italian Dill" leans this way).
