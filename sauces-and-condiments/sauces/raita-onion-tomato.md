# Raita — Onion-Tomato

A cooling yogurt condiment, essentially pico de gallo folded into spiced yogurt. It's the foil to anything spicy or fried — especially good next to crispy gobi or a rich biryani.

## Ingredients

- 1 cup plain whole-milk yogurt
- 2–3 tbsp water or milk, to loosen
- ¼ cup onion, finely diced
- ¼ cup tomato, deseeded and finely diced
- 1 green chili, minced (optional)
- 2 tbsp cilantro, chopped
- ½ tsp roasted cumin, ground (see step 1)
- ¼ tsp black salt (kala namak), or ½ tsp chaat masala
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- Pinch cayenne or Kashmiri chili, for color
- Salt, to taste
- Pinch sugar, optional, to balance

## Instructions

1. Roast the cumin: toast cumin seeds in a dry pan until dark and fragrant, cool, and grind. This roasted cumin (bhuna jeera) is what makes raita taste like raita instead of seasoned yogurt — it's worth doing fresh.
2. Whisk the yogurt smooth with the water or milk until it's thick but pourable, with no lumps.
3. Prep the vegetables. Always deseed the tomato. If making ahead, salt the diced onion and tomato lightly, rest 5 minutes, then drain — this keeps the raita from going watery.
4. Combine. Stir the onion, tomato, chili, and most of the cilantro into the yogurt. Add the roasted cumin, black salt or chaat masala, black pepper, cayenne, and salt. Taste and adjust — it should land tangy, savory, lightly funky, with a touch of heat.
5. Chill and garnish. Rest 15–20 minutes cold to let the flavors come together. Top with the remaining cilantro and a dusting of roasted cumin, and serve cold.

## Notes

- Watery raita is the most common failure: deseed the tomato and drain the salted vegetables.
- The black salt is doing the heavy lifting — that savory, faintly sulfurous funk against the cool yogurt is the point. Chaat masala stands in fine, since it already contains kala namak.
- Thin with a little more milk for a drizzle-able sauce, or leave it thick for a dip.
