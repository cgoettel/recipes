# Masoor Dal (Red Lentil)

Quick everyday dal. ~25 minutes start to finish. Best effort-to-reward ratio in home cooking — no soak, lentils break down on their own, the tadka does all the aromatic work.

Serves 3–4 with rice.

## Lentils
- 1 cup red lentils (masoor dal dhuli), rinsed until the water runs mostly clear
- 4 cups water
- 1 tsp turmeric
- ½ tsp salt (more to taste at the end)

## Tadka
- 2 tbsp ghee
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 1 tsp brown mustard seeds (optional but good)
- 2 dried red chiles, broken in half
- pinch of hing (asafoetida)
- 8–10 fresh curry leaves (if available)
- 3 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- ½ tsp Kashmiri chile or paprika

## Finish
- juice from ½ lemon
- cilantro

## Method

Combine lentils, water, turmeric, and salt in a pot. Bring to a boil, skim the foam in the first few minutes, then drop to a gentle simmer. Cook 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils have fully broken down into a loose porridge. Add water if it tightens too much — you want it pourable, not pasty.

Just before serving, make the tadka. Heat ghee in a small pan over medium-high. Add cumin and mustard seeds; they should sizzle and pop within seconds. Add the dried chiles, hing, and curry leaves (careful, the curry leaves will spit). Then the sliced garlic. As soon as the garlic is golden — 15–20 seconds, no more — pull off the heat and stir in the Kashmiri chile.

Pour the entire hot tadka over the dal. Stir half of it in, leave the rest swirled on top so you get pockets of intense spice. Finish with lemon and cilantro.

## Notes

- The tadka is the entire flavor system. Don't skip it, and don't make it ahead — it should hit the dal hot.
- Hing (asafoetida) is in small jars at any Indian grocery. Lasts forever. Adds a subtle savory/oniony note that's hard to replicate. Optional but worth having around.
- For a richer version: while the lentils simmer, cook a finely diced onion in ghee until golden, add 1 tbsp grated ginger and a chopped tomato, cook until jammy, then stir into the dal before the tadka.
- Eats like congee — soupy, comforting, standalone meal with rice. Day 2 is even better.

## Serve With
- Basmati rice (jeera rice is the classic pairing)
- Yogurt
- Something pickled
