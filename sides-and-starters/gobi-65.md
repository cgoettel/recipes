# Gobi 65

Dry, crispy South Indian–style fried cauliflower — the fried starter that shows up at most Indian restaurants. Florets get a thin, well-spiced cornstarch-and-rice-flour coating, fry up shatter-crisp, then get tossed in a quick curry-leaf-and-chili temper. It stays crunchy rather than going saucy; serve hot, right out of the pan, with a cooling raita.

## Ingredients

### Cauliflower and coating

- 1 medium head cauliflower, cut into small bite-size florets (~1½", uniform)
- ¼ cup cornstarch
- 3 tbsp rice flour
- 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste (or 1" ginger + 4 cloves garlic, grated)
- 1–1½ tsp Kashmiri chili powder (color and mild heat)
- ½ tsp cayenne, or to taste
- ½ tsp turmeric
- ½ tsp ground cumin
- ¾ tsp salt
- ⅓–½ cup water, to a thick clingy batter
- Neutral oil, for deep frying

### Temper and toss

- 2 tbsp oil (reuse some of the frying oil)
- 1 sprig curry leaves (~12 leaves)
- 2 green chilies, slit lengthwise
- 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
- ½ small onion, slivered (optional)
- 1 tsp chaat masala
- Juice of ½ lime
- Handful cilantro, chopped

## Instructions

1. Par-cook the florets for crisp insurance: boil in salted water 3 minutes, just enough to take the raw edge off so they cook through before the coating over-browns. Drain and dry thoroughly on a towel — surface water is what keeps the coating from crisping.
2. Make the batter. Whisk together the cornstarch, rice flour, all-purpose flour, ginger-garlic, both chili powders, turmeric, cumin, and salt. Add water a little at a time until it's a thick batter that clings to a floret like loose pancake batter, not runny. Fold in the cauliflower until every piece is coated. The rice-flour-and-cornstarch combo is the crisp; all-purpose alone goes leathery.
3. Fry hot. Heat oil to 350–375°F and fry in uncrowded batches until deep golden and crisp, 4–5 minutes. Drain on a wire rack rather than paper towels, which steam the bottoms soft. For extra hold, fry once at 350°F, rest, then flash-fry again at 375°F just before serving.
4. Temper. In a wide pan, heat 2 tbsp oil. Add the curry leaves (stand back — they spit), green chili, ginger-garlic, and onion if using. Toss 30–60 seconds until fragrant and the raw garlic smell is gone, then kill the heat.
5. Toss and finish. Off the heat, add the fried gobi and toss quickly to coat — keep it fast and dry so nothing softens. Finish with chaat masala, a squeeze of lime, and cilantro. Serve immediately, with onion-tomato raita alongside.

## Notes

- This is a right-out-of-the-pan dish. It holds maybe 15 minutes before the temper's moisture softens it.
- For a saucier Chilli Gobi instead, after the temper add 1 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp chili-garlic sauce, 1 tsp vinegar, and a pinch of sugar loosened with 1 tsp cornstarch in 2 tbsp water; toss until glazed. You trade crunch for stickiness.
- A beaten egg worked into the batter (cut the water to compensate) gives a denser, more classic "65" shell.
