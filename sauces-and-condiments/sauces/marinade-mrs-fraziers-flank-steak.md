# Marinade — Mrs. Frazier's Flank Steak

From Mrs. Frazier, whose original recipe card carried the instruction "(do not give out this recipe)." Written for flank steak, but it is arguably even better on chicken — thighs or a spatchcocked bird especially, where the sugar in the teriyaki has skin to caramelize against.

Makes enough for 2–3 lbs of meat.

## Ingredients

- ½ C Kikkoman teriyaki barbecue sauce
- ½ C soy sauce
- ½ C lemon juice
- 3–5 shakes Worcestershire sauce
- ¼ C vegetable oil

For the meat, before it goes in:

- Granulated garlic
- Salt
- Fresh cracked pepper

## Instructions

1. Season the meat first, dry, before any liquid touches it — coat all sides generously with granulated garlic, salt, and pepper. This matters: seasoning applied directly sticks to the surface and seasons the meat itself, where the same amount stirred into the marinade would just dilute into a quart of liquid and mostly get poured down the drain. Granulated garlic (not fresh, not powder) is the right texture here — it clings without scorching on the grill the way minced fresh garlic does.
2. Whisk or shake the teriyaki, soy sauce, lemon juice, Worcestershire, and oil together. The oil will not stay emulsified; shake it again right before it goes over the meat so the fat coats everything on the way in.
3. Pour over the seasoned meat in a zip-top bag or a dish, press out the air so the meat stays submerged, and refrigerate **3 hours**. Flip once at the halfway mark if the meat isn't fully covered.
4. Pull the meat out and let the surface drip off, then pat it dry — a wet surface steams instead of searing, and this marinade is wet and sugary. Discard the used marinade (see Notes if you want to baste).
5. Grill hot and fast over direct heat. Flank steak: 3–4 minutes per side for medium-rare, then rest 10 minutes and slice thin **across the grain** — flank is aggressively grainy and slicing with it turns a tender steak into rope. Chicken: sear over direct heat to get color, then finish over indirect until it hits temperature, so the sugar doesn't burn before the inside is done.

## Notes

- **Do not stretch the marinating time.** Three hours is a ceiling, not a suggestion. Between the soy sauce and the lemon juice this is both very salty and quite acidic; left overnight the acid denatures the outer layer of the meat and it goes chalky and mealy — a real risk on chicken, which is thinner and takes it faster than flank. Three hours is enough to season deeply without curing it.
- **Sugar burns.** The teriyaki brings sugar, which is where the lacquered exterior comes from, but it also means the surface will go from browned to black fast. Two-zone fire, or be ready to move things off the hot side.
- **If you want to baste**, reserve some marinade *before* it touches raw meat, or boil the used marinade a full minute first. Either way, stop basting early enough that the meat cooks on both sides afterward.
- **Scaling**: the ratio is the recipe — equal parts teriyaki, soy, and lemon juice, with half that much oil. Scale the three-part base up or down together and it holds.
- Bottled lemon juice works and is what makes this a weeknight recipe, but fresh-squeezed is noticeably brighter if there are lemons around.
