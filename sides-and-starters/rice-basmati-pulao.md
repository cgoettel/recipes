# Light Basmati Pulao

Perfumed basmati with whole warm spices. More aromatic than [jeera rice](rice-jeera.md) — what you want next to the [coconut chicken curry](../entrees/curries/coconut-chicken-curry.md) or any dish where you want the rice to actively contribute aroma rather than just sit there.

Not a full pulao with vegetables/meat — just the fragrant base.

Serves 2–3.

## Ingredients
- 1 cup basmati rice (aged Indian — India Gate, Tilda, Daawat, Lal Qilla)
- 1¼ cups water (after soaking; see Method)
- 1 tsp ghee
- 2–3 green cardamom pods, bruised (crack with the side of a knife)
- 1 small cinnamon stick
- 2 cloves
- 1 bay leaf
- ½ tsp salt

## Method

Rinse the rice in cool water, swirling and pouring off, until the water runs mostly clear — usually 5–7 changes. Then soak in cool water for 20–30 minutes. Drain well.

Add the drained rice, water, ghee, all the whole spices, and salt to the Zojirushi. Run the white rice setting. Fluff gently when done.

Pick out the bigger pieces as you serve, or warn whoever eats it — biting into a clove or cardamom pod is unpleasant.

## Notes

- **Water ratio matters.** Zojirushi water lines are calibrated for Japanese short-grain. For soaked basmati, ~1:1.25 by volume. Ignore the line.
- The aromatics here are subtle — they infuse the rice during cooking but don't dominate. If you want it more pronounced, you can lightly toast the whole spices in the ghee in a small pan for 20–30 seconds before adding everything to the cooker.
- Cardamom pods should be cracked open (not crushed flat) so the seeds inside can release. Whack with the flat of a knife.
- Saffron upgrade for special occasions: bloom 6–8 threads in 2 tbsp warm milk for 10 minutes, drizzle over the cooked rice, gently fold for a streaky gold effect. Mughlai move. Overkill for a Tuesday.
