# Bread — Artisan in Five (Master Recipe)

From [Artisan Bread in Five Minutes a Day](https://artisanbreadinfive.com/). The master recipe — refrigerator-aged dough that keeps two weeks, developing sourdough notes over time.

## Ingredients

- 3 C (1½ lbs) lukewarm water (cold water works but takes longer; never hot — you'll kill the yeast)
- 1 T granulated yeast
- 1–1½ T Morton kosher salt (adjust to taste)
- 6½ C (2 lbs) all-purpose flour

## Instructions

### Mixing the dough

1. In a 5- or 6-quart bowl or lidded dough bucket, combine water, yeast, and salt. No need to dissolve the yeast first — we're mixing the flour in quickly.
1. Dump in the flour all at once and stir with a long-handled wooden spoon or Danish dough whisk until all flour is incorporated. The dough will be wet and rough — no kneading required.
1. Cover with a lid (not snapped shut — yeast gases need to escape; a small hole in the lid works). Let sit at room temperature ~2 hours until risen and starting to collapse.
1. The dough can be used immediately, but is much easier to handle when chilled. Refrigerate up to two weeks — flavor deepens with time.

### Baking a loaf

1. Dust the surface of the dough with flour. Cut off a 1-pound piece with kitchen shears and form into a ball. Place on parchment or a cornmeal-dusted peel.
1. Let rest at least 40 minutes (60–90 for a more open crumb). The loaf may spread sideways rather than rise — that's normal.
1. Preheat oven to 450°F with a baking stone on the center rack and a metal broiler tray on the bottom (never glass — it will shatter; keep the tray 4–5 inches below the stone).
1. Slash the loaf with ¼-inch deep cuts using a very sharp serrated knife.
1. Slide the loaf onto the preheated stone. Pour a cup of hot water into the broiler tray to create steam.
1. Bake 30–35 minutes until deep brown. If using parchment, remove after 20–25 minutes and finish directly on the stone.
1. Cool fully on a rack before cutting — a hot cut yields a gummy interior.

## Notes

- **Refrigerator-rise trick**: shape the loaves and refrigerate overnight. In the morning they'll have risen and are ready for the oven after a brief room-temp rest while it preheats.
- Leftover bread keeps best uncovered, cut-side down on the counter — wrapping a crusty loaf makes it soggy.
