# Chiles Güeros Rellenos de Camarón

Yellow chiles stuffed with shrimp and melty cheese, capeado-style and fried until golden. Sinaloan coast on a plate. Serves 4.

## Ingredients

- 8 chiles güeros (or Hungarian wax peppers)
- 1 lb shrimp, peeled, deveined, chopped small
- 1 medium white onion, finely diced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 roma tomatoes, finely diced
- ¼ cup cilantro, chopped
- 1 tsp Mexican oregano
- 8 oz Oaxaca or Monterey Jack cheese, cut into strips
- 2 tbsp olive oil (for the filling)
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 4 large eggs, separated
- ¼ cup all-purpose flour (for dredging)
- 2 cups neutral oil (for frying)

## Instructions

> 🌶️ Chiles güeros run mild-to-medium with variable heat — taste one before committing. If they're scorchers, soak the peeled chiles in salted water for 10 minutes to tame them. You can also skip the capeado entirely and just pan-fry the stuffed chiles for a faster home-style version.

1. Char the chiles directly over a gas flame or under the broiler, turning until the skins blister all over — avoid blackening to mush, you want the flesh intact. Drop them into a bowl, cover, and let sweat for ~10 minutes. Peel off the skins, cut a lengthwise slit, and scoop out the seeds and ribs. Keep the stem on.
1. Heat the olive oil in a skillet over medium. Sweat the onion with a pinch of salt until translucent, then add garlic for 30 seconds. Add tomatoes and cook until they lose the raw edge and the pan looks jammy. Stir in shrimp and oregano, season with the rest of the salt, and cook just until the shrimp turn opaque — 2–3 minutes max. They'll keep cooking inside the chile. Off heat, fold in cilantro. Cool slightly.
1. Tuck a strip of cheese into each chile, then spoon in the shrimp filling — generous but not bursting. Press the slit closed; a toothpick helps if they rebel.
1. Whip the egg whites with a pinch of salt to stiff peaks, then beat the yolks in one at a time just until incorporated — you want a thick, glossy cloud that holds shape on a spoon. Spread the flour on a plate.
1. Heat the frying oil in a deep skillet to about 350°F. Dredge each chile lightly in flour, dunk in the egg batter to coat fully, and lower into the oil seam-side up. Fry 1–2 minutes per side, spooning hot oil over the tops to puff them. Pull when deep golden and drain on a rack or paper towels.
1. Eat immediately — they're best the moment the cheese is molten. Bathe in a light tomato caldillo (blended tomato, onion, garlic, simmered in broth), or serve with rice, beans, and a squeeze of lime.
