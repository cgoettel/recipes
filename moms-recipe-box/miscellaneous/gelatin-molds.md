# Gelatin molds

A reference sheet on layering and decorating fruit gelatin molds. Gelatin dissolves in hot liquid and cools to a gel of almost any shape.

## Ingredients

- Flavored gelatin (such as Jell-O), prepared according to package directions
- Fruit (see the notes for which kinds sink and which float)

## Instructions

### Clear layered mold

1. Prepare each flavor of gelatin according to package directions.
2. Chill each layer until almost firm before adding the next. If the first layer is too firm when the second is added, the layers may slip apart when unmolded.

### Cubes and jewels

1. For cubes, pour gelatin about 1 inch deep in a pan. When set, cut into small cubes or flake with a spoon.
2. For jewels, press set gelatin through a coarse strainer or mash with a fork.

### Special designs

1. Pour about ⅛ inch of gelatin into the mold and chill until set but not firm.
2. Cool the remaining gelatin. Arrange the design on the set layer, then cover carefully with a few spoonfuls of cooled gelatin to anchor it. Chill until set but not firm.
3. Pour the remaining cooled gelatin into the mold.

### Self-layering mold

1. Use some fruit from both lists below. The fruits separate into two distinct layers with clear gelatin between.

## Notes

- **Fruits that sink:** canned fruit packed in heavy syrup (apricots, Bing cherries, peaches, pears, pineapple, plums, whole cranberry sauce), cooked prunes, and fresh grapes (though grapes tend to float).
- **Fruits that float:** canned mandarin oranges; frozen blueberries (most float, very few sink), grapefruit, raspberries, peaches (these tend to sink), melon balls, and strawberries; fresh apples, bananas, grapefruit, melon, oranges, peaches, pears, and strawberries.
- Bing cherries turn the gelatin dark purple.
