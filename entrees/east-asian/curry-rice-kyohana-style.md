# Curry Rice (Kyohana-Style)

Rebuilt from a meal at Kyohana, a restaurant in Kyoto, and from watching how it was made. No boxed roux bricks. The curry's body comes from a deeply browned giblet stock, rich enough in collagen that leftovers set to a firm jelly in the fridge. If yours does that, you got it right.

The method has three stages, and each one adds depth: browning the giblets for fond, simmering a gelatin-heavy stock, and building a fresh roux with spices you toast yourself. Start the stock a day ahead. Curry is better on day two anyway.

Serves 4–6.

## Ingredients

### Giblet stock (makes about 2 L)

- 1–1½ lb chicken giblets and necks (gizzards, hearts, necks; leave out the liver, which turns bitter and cloudy)
- 1–1½ lb chicken feet, wings, or backs (feet are the collagen powerhouse; use at least some)
- 1 Tbsp neutral oil
- 1 onion, halved, skin on
- 2" piece ginger, sliced
- 4–6 garlic cloves, smashed
- Green tops of a bunch of green onions
- About 3 L cold water

### Spice blend

- 2 Tbsp coriander seed
- 1 Tbsp cumin seed
- 1 tsp fennel seed
- ½ tsp fenugreek seed
- Seeds from 4–5 green cardamom pods
- 1 tsp black peppercorns
- 1–2 dried red chilies, or ½–1 tsp cayenne
- 1 Tbsp ground turmeric
- 1 bay leaf

### Curry

- 2–3 large yellow onions, thinly sliced
- 2 Tbsp neutral oil
- 1½ lb chicken thighs, boneless, cut into large bite-size pieces
- 3–4 garlic cloves, grated
- 1" piece ginger, grated
- 1 Tbsp tomato paste
- 6 Tbsp butter
- ½ cup flour
- 1–1½ L giblet stock
- 1–2 Tbsp soy sauce
- 1 Tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- Salt to taste
- Optional: 1–2 carrots and 1–2 Yukon Gold potatoes, in large chunks
- Steamed short-grain rice, to serve

## Instructions

### Stock (the day before)

1. Pat the giblets and necks dry. Heat the oil in a heavy stockpot over medium-high heat and brown them in batches without crowding. Leave them alone until they release from the pot. You want a dark brown fond across the bottom, not a pale one. This is where most of the curry's depth comes from.
2. Put the halved onion cut-side down in the same pot and char it hard.
3. Splash in a cup of water and scrape up every bit of fond.
4. Add the feet, wings, or backs, the ginger, garlic, green-onion tops, and enough cold water to cover by an inch or two.
5. Bring slowly to a bare simmer and skim the gray foam that rises in the first 20 minutes. Keep it at a lazy bubble, never a boil. Boiling emulsifies the fat and clouds the stock, and gentle heat pulls out more gelatin. Simmer 4–6 hours, topping up with water if the bones poke through.
6. Strain, cool, and refrigerate overnight. It should set firm. If it's loose, reduce it by a third and test again. Lift off the fat cap and save a couple of spoonfuls for browning the chicken.
7. Optional: pick the gizzards and hearts out of the strainer, dice them small, and stir them into the finished curry for texture.

### Spice blend

1. Toast the whole seeds, peppercorns, chilies, and bay leaf in a dry skillet over medium heat, shaking the pan, until fragrant and a shade darker (2–3 minutes). Don't let the fenugreek go past light brown, or it turns bitter.
2. Cool, then grind fine and stir in the turmeric.

### Curry

1. Caramelize the onions: cook them in the oil over medium to medium-low heat for 45–60 minutes, stirring more often as they darken, until deep mahogany and jammy. Deglaze with a splash of stock whenever the bottom starts to catch. Don't rush this. These onions supply the sweetness that bricks get from sugar.
2. Meanwhile, season the chicken with salt. Brown it hard in a separate skillet using the saved chicken fat or a little oil, then set it aside. Deglaze that skillet with stock and pour it into the onions.
3. Add the garlic, ginger, and tomato paste to the onions. Cook 2–3 minutes, until the paste darkens.
4. Make the roux in a separate small pan: melt the butter, whisk in the flour, and cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, until it smells nutty and turns peanut-butter brown (10–15 minutes). Take it off the heat and stir in 3–4 Tbsp of the spice blend. The heat of the roux blooms the spices without scorching them.
5. Warm 1 L of the stock in the onion pot. Whisk in the roux a ladle at a time until smooth.
6. For a smooth, shop-style sauce, blend now with an immersion blender. For a rustic one, leave the onions in.
7. Add the chicken, plus the carrots and potatoes if using. Simmer gently, uncovered, 30–45 minutes, stirring the bottom often since roux-thickened sauces scorch. Thin with more stock as it tightens. It should coat a spoon thickly.
8. Season with soy sauce and Worcestershire, then salt. Taste for spice and add more of the blend if it needs it. Simmer 5 more minutes after any addition.
9. Serve over rice, or cool and refrigerate overnight. It will set to jelly and taste better reheated.

## Notes

- **The jelly test.** Collagen from the feet and wings is what makes the curry set when cold. If it doesn't set, use more feet next time or reduce the stock further. Reheat gently and loosen with a splash of water.
- **Shortcut spices.** S&B curry powder (the red can) can stand in for the homemade blend. It's still far better than bricks, and it includes cinnamon and clove.
- **Related.** The [Kaga bōcha curry](curry-rice-kaga-bocha.md) uses the same roux method with roasted tea in the liquid. This stock is the best base for it too.
